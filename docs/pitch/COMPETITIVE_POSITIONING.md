# ATHLIX — competitive positioning hypothesis

Public-source review: 13 November 2026. Provider feature descriptions below were
read from their own published pages; their products were not independently
benchmarked. No price, market-share, superiority, or exclusivity claim is made.

| Alternative | Published focus | Implication for ATHLIX |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smoothcomp | Event registration and organizer bracket creation, editing, publishing, and print workflows. | Event software is an established category. ATHLIX must prove a useful local workflow rather than claim it invented connected registrations and brackets. |
| Sportdata / SET | Event-management, competition-result, timing/scoring, membership, and video-review products. | Official competition rules and federation-scale operations require substantial domain work. ATHLIX does not currently establish equivalent coverage. |
| TeamUp | Customer records, classes/appointments, memberships, attendance, and payment features. | General club administration already has capable alternatives. A full academy CRM should follow demonstrated demand and completed core flows. |
| Forms, spreadsheets, and messaging | Proposed substitute to investigate with local organizers; no customer research was supplied. | Measure actual switching friction, cost, and failure modes instead of assuming a universal pain point. |

## Proposed wedge, with clear tradeoffs

Test a supervised karate-club/local-event workflow in one reachable geography.
Potential value is a clear athlete account-to-registration-to-result journey,
with optional coach-reviewed kata feedback. This is an inference about a useful
direction, not a capability advantage demonstrated against competitors.

ATHLIX currently has incomplete academy operations, no paid checkout, no
independent AI evaluation, limited field evidence, and ongoing security work.
Competitor breadth should inform integration or referral decisions rather than
drive an unnecessary rewrite of every feature.

## What might become defensible

Consented, longitudinal, coach-reviewed training observations could improve the
feedback loop if they prove useful to athletes. Repeated organizer usage could
produce workflow knowledge and trust. Neither dataset nor network effect is
established. Do not describe MediaPipe or a generic language-model integration
alone as a proprietary model, trained asset, or moat.

Interview organizers about their current tools, observe an event, compare task
completion and recovery behavior, and document why a buyer would switch. Revisit
this positioning after evidence exists; do not claim to be the only platform.
